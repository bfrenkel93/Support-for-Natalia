A simpler way to help

Grief is overwhelming.
Supporting someone shouldn’t be.

In the days and months after a loss, friends and family often want to help. But coordinating that help can become another responsibility for the very people who have the least capacity to manage it.

A support page gives your community one thoughtful place to show up.

Bring a Meal

Coordinate meals and practical help without endless group texts or overlapping plans.

Spend Time Together

Organize visits, outings, childcare, school pickups, or time with the kids.

Help With What Matters

Create specific needs your community can step into, from groceries and errands to appointments and everyday logistics.

Share a Memory

Collect photographs, stories, voice notes, and memories of the person who died, creating something their family can return to for years.

Share Stories for Their Family

Invite friends and family to share the stories and small details worth keeping — especially the ones the children may be too young to remember.

What were they like at 25? What always made them laugh? What story should they know someday?

On each page, this can be pointed right at the children — or held for the whole family. Some stories are worth saving before they fade.

There is no perfect thing to say.

But there are things we can do.

Bring dinner.

Take the kids somewhere fun.

Sit beside someone.

Handle the thing they shouldn’t have to think about.

Tell a story they haven’t heard before.

Remember the birthday.

Keep showing up after everyone else has gone home.

Grief doesn’t end after the first few weeks. Support shouldn’t either.

Built for the long after

Most people are surrounded by support immediately after a loss.

Then life moves forward.

The texts slow down.

The meals stop arriving.

Everyone returns to their routines.

The grieving family doesn’t.

Create support that can continue for weeks, months, even years — so the people who love them can keep showing up in meaningful ways.

The family shares what’s ahead

They can post the moments still to come — a birthday, a school game, a hard anniversary, a week that needs extra hands — so people always know how and when to show up.

Everyone can subscribe for updates

Friends and family opt in to occasional, gentle notes — no noise, unsubscribe anytime — so the circle stays close for years, not just the first few weeks.

One page. A whole community.

Create a private home for the people surrounding a grieving family.

Share the page only with the people you choose. Let friends and family sign up for what they can genuinely offer, while giving the family space to receive support without having to coordinate all of it themselves.

No one person has to do everything.
Everyone can do something.
